# Examples

Four annotated examples, written for this repo. They exist for one reason: **`VAULT.md` enforces the quality bar by pointing at concrete notes**, not by describing one. Strip the examples and the agent has nothing to calibrate against, and output drifts toward summary — the exact failure the standard is written to prevent.

| File | Type | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| `notes/example-concept-note.md` | concept | The density bar, filled in with real content. A core claim stated as an argument, a mechanism diagram, numbers in tables, genuine open questions |
| `notes/example-podcast-note.md` | podcast | The `foreign` state as captured, plus what to extract from long-form audio: exact quotes, spoken numbers, refusals, contradictions with existing notes |
| `notes/example-person-note.md` | person | **Positions they have publicly revised** — the section that makes person notes worth keeping, and the one nobody thinks to create |
| `threads/example-thread-ai-safety.md` | thread | The four-section structure, and what makes a dated observation worth writing |

The concept note carries real, verifiable content (Chinchilla scaling) because a note full of placeholders cannot demonstrate density, which is the whole point. The other three are annotated skeletons — inventing a real researcher's revised positions, or a real guest's concessions, would be worse than showing the shape.

## Three things to notice

**1. "Core claim" is an argument, not a description.** Every note opens with a thesis you could disagree with. Compare to what a summariser produces — "this paper discusses scaling laws" — which is unfalsifiable and therefore useless.

**2. "Open questions" are genuine.** Not comprehension checks. They are what the source left unresolved, and they are why a note is worth returning to. Several are phrased as "either X or Y, and the source doesn't say which."

**3. "Connections to existing notes" is where value compounds.** Each entry says what the connection *is*, not that one exists. This is the section that makes the corpus worth more than the sum of its notes — and the reason the backfill step in `VAULT.md` exists.

## The wikilinks don't resolve yet

The examples link to notes you have not written. In Obsidian they render greyed out, which is the correct impression: these are fragments cut out of a graph that does not exist yet.

That is also the honest demonstration. Link density *is* the standard — notes rewritten to resolve cleanly inside this folder would be tidy and unrepresentative.

## "My take" is empty, deliberately

In the podcast example it is empty because that is what a freshly captured note looks like: `status: foreign`, nobody has read it, and it sits in `READING-QUEUE.md` until someone writes a position.

This is the gate the whole system turns on. A note advances to `understood` when a human writes something here — not when they have read it, not when they feel they got it. If you cannot write the take, you did not understand it, which is unwelcome and correct.

The concept note shows a filled-in one. Note how short it is: two sentences naming the transferable lesson and a prior to carry forward. A good take is not a summary of the note — the note already did that. It is what *you* now think, and what you will do differently.

## Using them

Keep them until you have notes of your own worth pointing at. To replace them: pick two of your own notes, update the two filenames cited in `VAULT.md` under **Note quality standard**, *then* delete this folder — in that order. `VAULT.md` links these files by name, so deleting first leaves the quality standard pointing at nothing.
