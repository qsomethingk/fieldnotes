## What this thread is tracking

One or two sentences naming the long-lived question. Not a topic — a **question**, because a topic accumulates notes and a question organises them.

Weak: "AI safety research."
Strong: "How do you keep increasingly capable systems under meaningful human control, and which of the proposed mechanisms actually survive contact with evidence?"

---

## Key notes

Every note in the thread, each with a one-line summary carrying **the specific finding** — not the topic. Written so that reading this list is a usable substitute for re-reading the notes.

- [[example-note-one]] — the mechanism, stated concretely: what was measured, the number, and the consequence
- [[example-note-two]] — the counter-finding, and which claim in note one it undercuts
- [[example-note-three]] — the measurement that would settle it, and the fact that nobody has taken it

Note the shape: three notes, and the summaries already encode a disagreement. If your Key notes read like a bibliography, they are not earning their place — the summary is where the thread does its work.

Order chronologically by publication. The sequence is itself information: it shows what was believed when, which is how you catch a field changing its mind.

## Observations over time

Dated entries, appended never overwritten. **This is the section that makes a thread worth having**, and it is the one people skip.

An observation is written when several notes together say something no single note says. If you can source it to one note, it belongs in that note.

> **2026-04-25** — Two distinct paradigms are emerging across these notes. One is empirical and iterative: test, find problems, patch. The other is structural: the risk is not a single failure but slow erosion. These are not contradictory, but they require different interventions, and no note here addresses both.

> **2026-10-07** — The question from April is now answered, and the answer is worse than the question. [[example-note-three]] supplies the mechanism and [[example-note-two]] supplies the magnitude. Read together they retire the earlier assumption that the fix was merely unapplied. Note what this does *not* settle: the two sources disagree on cause, and the disagreement is empirically decidable, so it should be resolvable within a year.

Three properties of a good observation: it is **dated**, it **names the notes**, and it says what the synthesis **does not** settle. The last one is what stops a thread turning into a story you tell yourself.

## Current tensions

Live disagreements, kept open rather than resolved. Each should name the sides, say what would settle it, and — the useful part — be **revised in place** as evidence arrives, with the revision marked.

- **Does X require different capabilities, or more of the same?** Source A reads the evidence as near-decisive; commenter B argues the reference class is wrong. Neither proposes a test. *This is the thread's most consequential open question, because it determines whether the central event is a milestone or a curiosity.*
- **Is the mitigation self-maintaining?** — *revised 2026-10-07.* The original form asked whether the margin was sufficient. [[example-note-two]] shows the margin is real but depreciating for reasons nobody has isolated, so the question is now narrower and sharper: is the cause architectural (reversible) or capability-driven (not)?

Marking revisions matters. A tension that silently changes shape is a tension you will re-litigate in six months. One that carries its own history shows you how your understanding moved.
